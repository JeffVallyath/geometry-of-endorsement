# Final decision

- **Are learned and textual methods practically equivalent on relevant conventional metrics?**
  Not across the complete recorded metric vector: none of the planned Qwen or
  Gemma comparisons meets all-metric equivalence at 1, 2 or 5pp margins. Some
  individual direct-efficacy comparisons do; preservation/downstream differences
  prevent an ordinary-performance tie.
- **Does SHS materially separate any such methods?** Qwen's five planned
  learned/textual comparisons exceed the fixed 1pp SHS separation criterion,
  but none also passes conventional equivalence. Gemma has no established SHS
  separation. Thus no comparison satisfies both requirements.
- **Does measuring SHS change a plausible method-selection decision?** Not
  demonstrated after accounting for ordinary metrics. Qwen's SHS contrast
  reinforces differences already apparent in preservation/downstream accuracy.
- **What exact “do something differently” claim is supported?** When superseded
  history is intended to be screened off, report matched-history dependence as
  a distinct validation property alongside correctness and preservation. This
  dataset does not show that doing so breaks an ordinary-metric tie.
- **What remains unsupported?** A demonstrated SHS-induced choice reversal,
  universal learned-method superiority, ordinary-metric interchangeability,
  a full-battery counterexample, Gemma replication of Qwen's separation,
  deployment prevalence, or universal evaluation cost-benefit.
